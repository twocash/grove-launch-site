Where I come from, we're smart enough not to eat our own seed corn.

Two a16z partners went to the WSJ this week arguing open-weight models are how America beats China on AI. They're right about the threat. They're half-right about the cure.

Open weights solve the capability half. The consumption half — where every query, every reasoning trace, every approval pattern, every instance of human judgment flows outward to a vendor — reproduces the same dependency ratchet automatically. An open-weight model consumed through a centralized API captures the same seed corn a closed one does.

Human judgment is the seed corn. Every major AI consumer platform is structurally designed to harvest it. That's not a bug in any one vendor. It's the business model.

95% of enterprise AI pilots produce zero measurable ROI. It's not a demand problem. It's what happens when the consumption pattern treats user judgment as exhaust instead of capital.

The WSJ piece missed a $30–40 billion stranded venture opportunity: the reverse-ratchet software layer that turns the dependency dynamic 180 degrees. It maps directly onto central Indiana — where compliance-heavy industries have been pricing unauthorized automation for a century.

Open weights are necessary. Sovereignty primitives are what make them sufficient.
