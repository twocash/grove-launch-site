II

What Andon Is

Andon is a discipline from the Toyota Production System — a signal mechanism on the manufacturing line. Sakichi Toyoda’s 1924 automatic loom introduced jidoka: the machine detects abnormality, halts, and surfaces the issue to a human for judgment. The andon system gave that signal a public surface — a colored light visible across the floor. Green: running. Yellow: supervised, call for help. Red: line stopped.

The discipline is structural humility: the system declares what it cannot decide on its own, and asks for the right person at the right moment.

This page is yellow. The system is running. Some surfaces require human judgment to access; the gate is honored on social grounds, not technical ones.

Kaizen

Kaizen — continuous improvement through small structural refinements — runs through the Toyoda lineage Grove builds on. This page ships low-fi: a yellow andon light, plain prose, an email address. It is discipline rather than feature. It improves through use. Surfaces that need to compose with it surface; surfaces that don’t, don’t.
